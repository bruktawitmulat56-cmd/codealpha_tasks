01 — What is phishing?

Tricking people, not systems

Phishing is a fake email, message, or website designed to trick someone into handing over credentials, money, or sensitive data.

It targets human psychology — not a software bug. That's what makes it so hard to patch away.

02 — Why it works

Three psychological triggers

  • Urgency — "act now or lose access"
  • Authority — appears to come from IT, HR, or a trusted office
  • Familiarity — mimics emails you already expect

Attackers don't need to hack your system if they can convince you to open the door yourself.

03 — Email red flags

How to spot a fake email

  • Urgent or threatening tone
  • Sender address slightly off (e.g. support@paypa1.com)
  • Generic greeting ("Dear user")
  • Unexpected attachment or link
  • Asks you to "verify" or "confirm" credentials
04 — Website red flags

How to spot a fake page

  • Misspelled or unusual domain name
  • No HTTPS padlock, or a warning in the browser
  • Login page looks almost right, but slightly off
  • Redirects you somewhere strange after submitting

Rule: always check the URL before typing a password — not just the page design.

05 — Case study

I ran a real simulation

Using the GoPhish framework, I built a fake "Registrar's Office" login page and sent a personalized phishing email to test recipients — as a controlled, consented exercise.

Fake sender: Registrar's Office Fake urgency: registration deadline
06 — What happened

The results

50%opened the email
100%of openers clicked the link
100%of clickers submitted credentials
30sclick → credential submit

Once someone clicked, nobody paused to check the page — they just logged in.

07 — Defense

How to protect yourself

  • Hover over links before clicking — check where they really go
  • Verify the sender's actual email address
  • Turn on Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)
  • When unsure, go to the site directly — don't click the email link
  • Report suspicious emails instead of ignoring them
08 — Quick check

Real or fake?

support@paypa1-secure.com emails you saying your account will be suspended in 2 hours unless you "verify" by clicking a link.

Stay alert, stay safe

When in doubt — don't click. Verify through a separate, trusted channel.
